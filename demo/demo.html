General Dentistry

Most bad days start as small problems that were easy to miss. A cavity that sat too long. A filling that cracked and let germs in. Gums that bled when you brushed. At a general visit, the team checks for all of that. They look at your teeth and gums, take X-rays, clean your teeth, and ask about pain, about cold water that stings, and about teeth that feel loose.

That talk matters. Pain that lingers after a cold drink, pain that wakes you at night, or a bump on the gum can mean the soft tissue inside a tooth is hurt. A checkup can catch a deep cavity before it turns into a Sunday emergency, and a filling at that stage is far simpler than the work that comes later.

An Emergency dentist Palm Beach Gardens will tell you the same thing every dentist tells you: brush twice a day, floss each day, and keep your checkups, even when nothing hurts. Those plain habits stop most of the decay that turns into a weekend crisis.

Emergency

The first hour is the hour that counts. If a tooth comes out, hold it by the crown, the top part you chew with, not the root. Rinse it gently if it is dirty. Do not scrub it. Do not dry it with a towel. If you can, put it back in the socket and hold it with a clean cloth. If you cannot, keep it in milk, or tuck it in your cheek. Then get to the office fast. A tooth put back within the hour has a far better chance than one that sits dry all day.

If a tooth cracks, do not chew on that side. Rinse with warm salt water. Put a cold cloth on the outside of your cheek to keep swelling down. Do not put heat on your face. Take the pain pill you would take for a headache, if it is safe for you, and call the office. If a crown falls off, keep it, do not chew on that side, and do not try to glue it back with home glue.

What counts as an emergency? A tooth that is out, cracked, or broken. Pain that will not stop. Swelling in the face or jaw. Bleeding that will not stop. A crown or filling that fell off and left a sharp edge. If it hurts, if it bleeds, or if a tooth moved, call. The team will ask what happened, how much it hurts, and if there is swelling, then tell you how soon to come in.

Cosmetic Dentistry

A broken front tooth is more than pain. It is a smile you hide with your hand at work and in photos. Cosmetic care can fix that, once the hurt is past. A small break can be fixed with bonding, a tooth-colored material shaped to match the tooth. A larger break may need a crown, a cap that covers the tooth and is shaded to match the teeth around it.

If a front tooth is lost for good, an implant crown can be matched in color, size, and shape to the teeth next to it. Ask to see photos of past work. Ask how the shade is picked, and ask what happens if the crown does not look right to you. A team that takes time with shade and shape is a team that takes time with the rest of the work too.

Oral Surgery

Some urgent visits need surgery, in the plain sense of the word. A tooth broken below the gum line may need to come out. A tooth with a deep infection may need the tip of its root cleaned. Wisdom teeth that swell and ache can need to come out too. The team will take X-rays first, so they can see the root, the bone, and any infection, then talk you through the plan.

You will get a short care list for home. Rest that day. Eat soft food. Keep the area clean. Call if pain gets worse after a few days instead of better. Tell the team about every pill you take and every health issue, because blood thinners and diabetes change how a mouth heals, and the team can plan around them only if they know.

Dental Implants

What if a tooth cannot be saved? A root may be cracked down its length, or the tooth may be too broken to hold a crown. When that happens, the team will tell you straight, and they will talk about what comes next, once the pain is gone and the area has healed.

A dental implant is a small titanium post that takes the place of a tooth root, with a crown on top. It stands on its own, it stays put when you eat, and it helps keep the jawbone from shrinking away. It is not the first choice when a tooth can be saved, but it is a strong back-up plan, and it can fill the space for good after one bad day.

Invisalign®

Teeth that crowd together are harder to clean, and plaque that sits in tight spots can turn into decay. Teeth that bite unevenly can crack under pressure. Clear aligners can help with both, after the urgent work is done.

Invisalign® uses clear trays that move teeth a little at a time. You take them out to eat and brush, and you swap them every week or two. Straighter teeth are easier to brush and floss, and a bite that lines up spreads pressure in a fair way across your teeth. If your teeth crowd, or your bite feels off, ask about it at a later visit.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need the emergency room, or a dentist?
Heavy bleeding that will not stop, trouble breathing or swallowing, or a bad blow to the face or jaw needs the emergency room first. For a broken tooth, a knocked-out tooth, or tooth pain with swelling, call a dentist right away. A dentist has the X-rays and tools to fix the tooth itself.

How fast must a knocked-out tooth be put back?
Within the hour is best. Keep it moist in milk or in your cheek, hold it by the crown, and get to the office fast. Every minute it sits dry makes it harder to save.

Can I just wait and see if the pain goes away?
Pain that comes and goes can still grow fast. A small infection can spread, and a crack can get worse with one more bite. Call the office and let the team decide how soon you need to come in. A phone call costs nothing.

What happens at the visit?
The team takes X-rays, finds the sore tooth, and stops the pain first. That may mean a filling, a root canal, a crown, or taking a tooth out. They talk you through the plan and the cost before they start.

How much will it cost?
The cost shifts with the problem and the fix. A visit and X-rays cost less than a root canal or a crown. Ask for a written plan. The office works with many insurance plans and with payment plans like CareCredit and Cherry, so the cost can be spread out.

My crown fell off. What do I do?
Keep the crown, do not chew on that side, and call the office. Do not use home glue. The team can often put it back on, or make a new one if it is broken.

Can kids be seen for a dental emergency?
Yes. Kids break teeth in games and falls, and they need care fast too. Call the office, tell them what happened and how old your child is, and they will tell you how soon to come in.
